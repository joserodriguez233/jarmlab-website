## Research Overview

The **Rodríguez-Martínez Laboratory** seeks to understand how the information encoded in non-coding regions of the human genome is interpreted by transcription factors (TFs) — and how disruptions in this molecular dialogue contribute to cardiovascular disease and congenital disorders. We combine biochemistry, high-throughput genomics, and computational approaches to address these questions.

---

## Non-Coding Variant Interpretation

The vast majority of disease-associated genetic variants identified by genome-wide association studies (GWAS) fall in non-coding regions of the genome. We develop experimental and computational frameworks to evaluate how these variants alter transcription factor binding and downstream gene regulation.

We use high-throughput *in vitro* selection assays (SELEX-seq, EMSA), massively parallel reporter assays (MPRAs), and machine learning models trained on TF binding preferences to prioritize and validate variants that change regulatory activity. A central focus is understanding how single nucleotide variants at or near TF binding motifs modulate binding affinity in a quantitative, predictable manner.

**Key tools & approaches:** SNP Bind-n-Seq · gapped k-mer SVMs (LS-GKM) · MPRA · fluorescence polarization / EMSA · protein purification and binding assays

---

## Transcription Factor DNA-Binding Biophysics

Transcription factors recognize their genomic binding sites through sequence-specific contacts with DNA. Yet the rules governing specificity — why a TF prefers one sequence over another, and how flanking sequence context modulates affinity — remain incompletely understood.

We dissect the biophysical basis of TF–DNA recognition, with an emphasis on cardiac TFs (NKX2-5, GATA4, TBX5) and bZIP family members. We characterize how disease-causing missense mutations in DNA-binding domains alter protein stability and binding affinity, and we map the specificity landscapes of TF homo- and heterodimers using high-throughput binding assays.

**Key tools & approaches:** Protein biochemistry · thermal shift assays · HTSELEX · SELEX-seq · structural bioinformatics

## Cardiovascular Disease Genomics

Congenital heart disease (CHD) and acquired cardiovascular disease are leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Genetic factors play a major role, but pinpointing the causal variants — particularly in non-coding regions — is a central challenge.

We integrate human genetics data (GWAS, eQTLs, rare variant studies) with functional genomic readouts (ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, Hi-C) and our TF binding measurements to identify and prioritize regulatory variants that mechanistically contribute to cardiac disease. We collaborate with clinical and epidemiological partners to bring these discoveries closer to clinical translation.

**Key diseases:** Congenital heart defects · coronary artery disease · atrial fibrillation
